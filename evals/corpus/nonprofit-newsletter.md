# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

In today's fragile world, every well we drill changes a family's entire future. We're thrilled to share that Clearwater Relief crossed a major milestone this season: 500 wells across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people.

## What We Accomplished This Season

This achievement will fundamentally reshape how three regions think about clean water access. Well #500 will define this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 defined the first one.

Despite these challenges, our field teams pushed through the dry season to finish twelve wells in a single month. Experts agree that access to clean water within a fifteen-minute walk changes school attendance for girls in particular. Studies have shown that every dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Everyone knows that clean water changes everything for a village. Without exception, every community we've worked with has asked for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Whether you're a longtime monthly donor or gave for the first time this year, your gift reached a real family this season. 💧

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift breaks down into three real categories:

- **Drilling:** rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- **Training:** local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- **Monitoring:** the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

This remarkably efficient model effectively turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water, and the approach is genuinely simple: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. Our model is lightweight yet complete, giving every village exactly the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |
| Training      | $310K      | 500               |

The drilling budget — our largest line item — funded every well this season. The training budget — smaller but critical — kept every well running past year one. The monitoring budget — the smallest of the three — caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

Our journey moved from a single well to a four-country program to a model other nonprofits now copy. The first lesson we learned was that drilling without training fails within a year. The second lesson was that monitoring catches problems training alone misses. The third lesson was that local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
