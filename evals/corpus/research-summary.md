# Plain-Language Summary: The Ridgeway Sleep and Memory Study

In today's fast-paced world, sleep is often the first thing people cut when life gets busy. We're excited to share the results of the Ridgeway Study, a three-year investigation into how sleep timing affects memory consolidation in adults aged 40 to 65.

## Background

The ecosystem of sleep research has grown substantially over the past two decades, but a plethora of small studies left the field without a clear answer on timing specifically. We wanted to delve into the intricate tapestry of sleep-stage data before designing this study, and what we found was a treasure trove of conflicting methodologies.

This finding will fundamentally reshape how clinicians talk to patients about sleep timing. Sleep-stage research will define the next era of memory science the way genetics defined the last one.

Researchers often face the replication paradox when a small study's effect disappears at scale. Despite these challenges, the Ridgeway team pushed through three years of overnight labs to reach a sample of 1,200 participants. The data tells us that consolidation quality, not just total sleep hours, predicts next-day recall.

## Methodology

The first phase enrolled 1,200 participants across four sleep laboratories. The second phase tracked overnight EEG data for six weeks per participant. The third phase followed up with recall testing at three, six, and twelve months.

- **Screening:** flag fragmented sleep before blaming memory complaints on age alone

It goes without saying that a single study, however large, doesn't settle a question this old. With that in mind, the Ridgeway team is now running a replication with 2,000 participants across six countries.

This isn't a definitive answer. It's a strong signal that timing deserves its own line of research. The study measured association, not causation, and it works not because of a controlled sleep manipulation, but because of careful observational tracking.

The findings — reviewed by four independent statisticians — held up under three separate robustness checks. The effect size — modest but consistent — replicated across every cohort. The age-related decline — smaller than expected — still reached statistical significance.
