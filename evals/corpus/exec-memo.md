# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

In today's competitive landscape, every quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster-moving rivals. I'm thrilled to present our Q3 priorities after two weeks of planning sessions across every team.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision will fundamentally reshape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

The team often faces the roadmap paradox when every group wants its project prioritized first. Despite these challenges, leadership pushed through a difficult planning cycle to land on three bets instead of ten. The market demands faster releases, even when our own infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

It's not about doing more. It's about doing the right three things. We picked these bets not just for revenue, but for the platform work they force us to finish.

Let's circle back to the metrics that matter and revisit them at every leadership sync this quarter. We need to take a step back and reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. The team should think outside the box on staffing instead of just hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

This groundbreaking approach provides transformative results, and our next-generation platform will seamlessly integrate every internal tool once the migration finishes. This best-in-class reliability push rests on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

The reliability workstream — owned by infrastructure — cuts across every product team. The platform workstream — owned by the core team — blocks two of the other three bets. The growth workstream — owned by product — depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports suggest that incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Experts agree that platform debt compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

The analysis suggests that it seems like our incident rate correlates more with deploy frequency than headcount. In the data, it appears that the correlation may hold across every team, though it's possible one team's numbers are skewing the average. Broadly, to some extent the sequencing bet could pay off by Q4, and one might say the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability first. Platform second. Growth third. No exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard. We staffed the platform team. We paused two lower-priority projects. We told customers what to expect.

The result? A calmer on-call rotation and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget functions as a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. The platform budget stands as the largest single line item this quarter. Headcount for growth serves as the last lever we'll pull, only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

This isn't a hiring freeze. It's a hiring pause on anything that isn't reliability or platform. We approved these budgets not just for this quarter, but for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first budget line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second budget line covers the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. The third budget line covers a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Everyone knows that budget season is always contentious. Without exception, every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

Whether you're on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. Here's the thing: we're not asking anyone to do more with less — we're asking every team to do less, better.

Look, I'm going to be honest about the tradeoff: some feature work slips to Q4 because of this. So here's what we're protecting instead — the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

What changes for engineering this quarter? Why does platform work come before growth? When will feature work resume at full speed? How will we know the bet paid off?

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

This remarkably disciplined plan effectively removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is genuinely simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real yet manageable, and painful yet temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

In conclusion, Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, and no scope creep. This isn't a pause on growth. It's the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

Make no mistake: this sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat. Let that sink in — three bets, one quarter, full team alignment. Full stop.

As we discussed at the offsite, low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't get us through the next stage of growth. Let's lean into the harder platform work now and double down on growth once it's finished.
