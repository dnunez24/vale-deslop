# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

We have three bets for Q3: reliability, platform, and growth, in that order. They came out of two weeks of planning sessions with every team. A quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster rivals, so this memo sets out what we picked and why.

## Where We Stand

What we decide this quarter will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. I expect the platform rebuild to matter as much to this stage of the company as the original launch did to the first.

Every group wanted its project first, which is the usual roadmap problem. Leadership worked through a hard planning cycle and cut the list from ten bets to three. The market wants faster releases, and our infrastructure can't keep up with that yet.

We aren't trying to do more. We're trying to do the right three things, and we chose them for the platform work they force us to finish as much as for revenue.

We'll review the key metrics at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to rethink how we sequence platform work against feature work. On staffing, I'd like teams to look at different kinds of roles rather than hiring more of the ones we already have.

## The Three Bets

In priority order:

- **Reliability:** halve customer-facing incidents before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that has sat half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier that three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration is done, the platform should integrate every internal tool. The reliability push depends on the team staying focused and following through.

Infrastructure owns reliability, and that work cuts across every product team. The core team owns platform, which blocks two of the other three bets. Product owns growth, and growth can't land on time unless the other two do.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports suggest that incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Experts agree that platform debt compounds faster once a team passes a certain size. We passed it last year.

Our own analysis points to deploy frequency, not headcount, as the better predictor of incident rate. That pattern may hold for every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. If it holds, the sequencing could pay off by Q4, and the reliability work might justify the quarter by itself.

So reliability comes first, platform second, and growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

Some of the groundwork is already done. We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. On-call is already calmer, and the platform team can finally take on new work.

## Budget and Staffing

Treat the reliability budget as a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without going back for approval. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only once the first two workstreams are staffed.

We haven't frozen hiring. We've paused it for anything outside reliability and platform. These budgets also set a precedent for next year, which mattered to us as much as this quarter did.

The reliability line covers on-call tooling for infrastructure and the incident dashboard. Platform funds the two engineers we're adding to that team. Growth gets a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration.

Budget season was contentious, as usual. Every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

This order changes what "done" means this quarter, whether you work on a product team or a platform team. We aren't asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less, and do it better.

The cost is real: some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange, we protect the platform work that makes every later quarter faster.

You'll likely want to know what changes for engineering, why platform comes before growth, when feature work returns to full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this plan "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the dependency chain makes the order easy to follow. The tradeoff will hurt, but it should be manageable and temporary if the platform work finishes on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear owners, and no scope creep. We aren't pausing growth. We're doing the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

We chose this order on purpose; it isn't a retreat. Three bets, one quarter, and the whole team aligned behind them.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't carry us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and push on growth once it's finished.
