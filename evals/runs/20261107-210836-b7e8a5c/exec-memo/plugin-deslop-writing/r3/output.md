# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter we spend without a clear bet is a quarter faster rivals take from us. After two weeks of planning sessions with every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where We Stand

What we decide this quarter will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will mark this stage of the company the way the original launch marked the first.

Every group wanted its project first on the roadmap, which is the usual problem. Leadership worked through a hard planning cycle anyway and cut the list from ten bets to three. The market wants faster releases, even though our own infrastructure can't keep up yet.

Doing more was never the goal. Doing the right three things is. We chose these bets for revenue, and also for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll review the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to rethink how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. On staffing, teams should try new approaches rather than hiring more of the roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents in half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that has sat half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier that three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the new platform will integrate every internal tool, and we expect the results to be large. The reliability push depends on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns reliability, which cuts across every product team. The core team has platform, and two of the other three bets can't move until it does. Growth belongs to product, and it depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports suggest that once incident rates rise above our current baseline, renewals start to suffer within two quarters. Experts agree that platform debt compounds faster after a team passes a certain size. We passed it last year.

Our analysis points to deploy frequency, more than headcount, as the thing our incident rate tracks. The correlation may hold for every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. The sequencing bet could pay off by Q4, and you could argue the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

The order is reliability, then platform, then growth, with no exceptions this quarter.

Some of this is already done. We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect.

So far that has bought us a calmer on-call rotation, and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

Treat the reliability budget as a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another round of approval. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

We are not freezing hiring. We are pausing any hiring outside reliability and platform. These budgets matter for this quarter and for the precedent they set going into next year.

Reliability money goes to infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. On platform, it covers the two engineers we're adding. Growth funds a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration.

Budget season is always contentious, and this one was no different: every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

Product team or platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We aren't asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less, and do it better.

The tradeoff is that some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange, we protect the platform work that makes every later quarter faster.

You probably have questions. What changes for engineering this quarter? Why does platform work come before growth? When will feature work return to full speed? How will we know the bet paid off?

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and once you see the dependency chain, the sequencing is simple. The tradeoff is real, and it will hurt, but we can manage it, and it stays temporary if the platform work finishes on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear owners, and no scope creep. We aren't pausing growth. We're doing the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

The sequencing is deliberate. It is not a retreat. Three bets, one quarter, and the whole team aligned behind them.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't carry us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and push hard on growth once it's finished.
