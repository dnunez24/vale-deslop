# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

We're making three bets in Q3, and this memo explains them. They came out of two weeks of planning sessions with every team. Each quarter we spend without a clear bet is a quarter that faster rivals take from us.

## Where We Stand

What we decide this quarter will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will matter to this stage of the company as much as the original launch mattered to the first.

Planning always runs into the same problem: every group wants its project first. Leadership worked through a hard cycle anyway and settled on three bets instead of ten. Customers expect faster releases than our infrastructure can support yet.

So the goal is the right three things, not more things. Revenue was one reason we chose these bets. The bigger reason is that they force us to finish the platform work.

We'll review the key metrics at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to rethink how we sequence platform work against feature work. On staffing, I'd like teams to look past hiring more people into the roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** halve customer-facing incidents before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that has sat half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier that three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration is done, every internal tool will run on the new platform. The reliability push depends on our staying focused and following through.

Infrastructure owns reliability, which touches every product team. The core team owns platform, and two of the other three bets are blocked until it ships. Product owns growth, and growth only ships on time if the other two do.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports suggest that once incident rates climb above our current baseline, renewals start to suffer within two quarters. Experts agree that platform debt compounds faster after a team passes a certain size. We passed it last year.

Our own analysis points to deploy frequency, more than headcount, as the driver of our incident rate. That pattern may hold for every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. If the sequencing works, it could pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone may be enough to justify the quarter.

The order is reliability, then platform, then growth, with no exceptions this quarter.

Some of this is already done. We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect.

On-call is calmer as a result, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without going back for approval. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. We'll add growth headcount last, and only once the first two workstreams are staffed.

We haven't frozen hiring. We've paused it for any role outside reliability and platform. These budgets cover this quarter, and they also set a precedent for next year.

The reliability line pays for infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. Platform's covers the two engineers we're adding, and growth's funds a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration.

Budget season is contentious every year. This quarter, every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

On a product team or a platform team, this order changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We aren't asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less, and do it better.

There is a cost: some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange, we protect the platform work that makes every later quarter faster.

You'll likely have four questions. What changes for engineering this quarter? Why does platform come before growth? When does feature work return to full speed? And how will we know the bet paid off?

Some team leads have called this order "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the order makes sense once you trace the dependencies. The tradeoff is real, and it will hurt. If the platform work finishes on schedule, it's also manageable and temporary.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear owners, and no scope creep. We aren't pausing growth. We're doing the platform work that next year's growth depends on.

This order is a deliberate choice, not a retreat. Three bets, one quarter, and the whole team aligned behind them.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't carry us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and push hard on growth once it's done.
