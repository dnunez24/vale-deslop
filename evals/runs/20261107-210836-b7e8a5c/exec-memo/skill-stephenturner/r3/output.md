# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Each quarter we spend without a clear bet gives faster rivals another quarter to pull ahead. After two weeks of planning sessions with every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where We Stand

What we decide this quarter will set how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will shape this period of the company much as the original launch shaped the first one.

Every group wanted its project at the top of the roadmap, and that made planning hard. Leadership worked through it and settled on three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases, and our infrastructure can't keep up with that pace yet.

We aren't trying to do more. We're trying to do the right three things, and we chose them for the revenue and also for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll review the metrics we care about at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to rethink how we've sequenced platform work against feature work. On staffing, I'd like teams to look past hiring more people into the roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push depends on the teams staying focused and following through.

Infrastructure owns the reliability workstream, which touches every product team. The core team owns the platform workstream, and it blocks two of the other three bets. Product owns growth, which can only land on time if the other two do.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports suggest that incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt is said to compound faster once a team passes a certain size, and we passed it last year.

Our analysis points to deploy frequency, more than headcount, as the thing our incident rate tracks. The correlation may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. If it holds, the sequencing could pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone might justify the quarter.

The order is reliability, then platform, then growth, and we won't make exceptions to it this quarter.

We've already shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, and paused two lower-priority projects. We also told customers what to expect.

Since then the on-call rotation has calmed down, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

Treat the reliability budget as a floor rather than a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. The platform budget is the largest single line item this quarter. We'll add growth headcount last, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

We haven't frozen hiring. We've paused hiring for anything outside reliability and platform, and we approved these budgets partly for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first budget line pays for infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on, and the third for a contractor on the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season usually gets contentious. This quarter, every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

Product team or platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We're asking every team to do less and do it better, which is different from asking you to do more with less.

The tradeoff is that some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange we protect the platform work that will make every later quarter faster.

You probably want to know what changes for engineering, why platform comes before growth, when feature work returns to full speed, and how we'll tell whether the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is easy to follow once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real but manageable, and if the platform work lands on schedule, the pain is temporary.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear owners, and no scope creep. We aren't pausing growth; we're doing the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

We chose this order on purpose, and it isn't a retreat. The whole team is aligned behind three bets for one quarter.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't carry us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and put more into growth once it's done.
