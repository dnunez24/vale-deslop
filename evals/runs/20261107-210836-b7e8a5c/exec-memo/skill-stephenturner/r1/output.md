# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Each quarter we go without a clear bet, faster rivals take ground from us. After two weeks of planning sessions with every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where We Stand

What we decide this quarter will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The original launch defined the company's first era, and I expect the platform rebuild to define this one.

When every group wants its project to go first, the roadmap gets stuck. Leadership worked through a hard planning cycle anyway and settled on three bets instead of ten. Customers want faster releases, and our infrastructure can't keep up with that pace yet.

We aren't trying to do more this quarter, just the right three things. We chose these bets for revenue and also because they force us to finish the platform work.

We'll review the key metrics at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to rethink how we've sequenced platform work against feature work. On staffing, I'd like teams to look past simply hiring more people into the roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the new platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push depends on the teams staying focused and following through.

Infrastructure owns the reliability workstream, which touches every product team. The core team owns the platform workstream, and two of the other three bets can't move until it's done. Product owns growth, which needs both of the others to land on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports suggest that once incident rates rise above our current baseline, they start costing renewals within two quarters. Experts agree that platform debt compounds faster after a team passes a certain size, and we passed it last year.

Our analysis points to deploy frequency, more than headcount, as the driver of our incident rate. The correlation may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. If it holds, the sequencing bet could pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone might justify the quarter.

So the order is reliability, then platform, then growth, with no exceptions this quarter.

We've already shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. On-call has gotten calmer, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

Infrastructure can spend up to the reliability budget without another approval round, so treat that number as a floor rather than a ceiling. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. We'll add growth headcount last, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

We're pausing hiring for anything outside reliability and platform, but this is not a hiring freeze. We also approved these budgets with next year in mind, since they set a precedent.

Reliability money goes to infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. Platform money pays for the two additional engineers joining that team. Growth money funds a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season was contentious, as usual. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

This sequencing changes what "done" means for product and platform teams alike. Nobody is being asked to do more with less. We're asking each team to do less and do it better.

The tradeoff is that some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange, we protect the platform work that will make every later quarter faster.

You probably want to know what changes for engineering, why platform comes before growth, when feature work picks back up to full speed, and how we'll judge whether the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the dependency chain makes the order easy to follow. The cost is real but manageable, and it stays temporary as long as the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is about discipline: three bets, clear owners, and no scope creep. We aren't pausing growth; we're doing the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

We chose this order on purpose, and it isn't a retreat. The whole team is aligned on three bets for one quarter.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far but won't carry us through the next stage. We'll take on the harder platform work now and put more into growth once it's finished.
