# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter we spend without a clear bet is a quarter faster rivals take from us. After two weeks of planning sessions with every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where We Stand

What we decide this quarter will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. I expect the platform rebuild to matter as much to this period of the company as the original launch mattered to the first.

When every group wants its project done first, the roadmap stalls. Leadership worked through a hard planning cycle anyway and cut the list from ten bets to three. The market wants faster releases, and our infrastructure can't keep up with that yet.

We chose three things over doing more. Revenue was one reason for picking these bets, but we also picked them because they force us to finish the platform work.

We'll review the metrics we care about at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to rethink how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. On staffing, I'd like teams to propose something other than hiring more of the roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the new platform will connect every internal tool. The reliability push depends on staying focused and following through.

Infrastructure owns the reliability workstream, which cuts across every product team. The core team owns the platform workstream, and it blocks two of the other three bets. Product owns growth, which depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports suggest that incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Experts agree that platform debt compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our analysis points to deploy frequency, more than headcount, as the driver of our incident rate. The correlation may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. If the sequencing works, it could pay off by Q4, and I think the reliability work alone would justify the quarter.

The order is reliability, then platform, then growth, with no exceptions this quarter.

So far we have shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. On-call is calmer already, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

Infrastructure can spend up to the reliability budget without another approval round; treat it as a floor rather than a ceiling. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

We are pausing hiring for anything outside reliability and platform, which is narrower than a freeze. We approved these budgets for this quarter and also for the precedent they set going into next year.

Reliability's money goes to infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. Platform's covers the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on, and growth's pays for a contractor on the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious, and this one was no different: every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

Whether you're on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We aren't asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less and do it better.

The honest tradeoff is that some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange we protect the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

You probably want to know what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and once you see the dependency chain the order makes sense. The tradeoff is real. It is also manageable, and if the platform work lands on schedule the pain will be temporary.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter, with three bets, clear owners, and no scope creep. Growth isn't on hold; the platform work is what makes next year's growth possible.

We chose this sequencing deliberately, and it is not a retreat. The whole team is aligned on three bets for one quarter.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't carry us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and put more into growth once it's finished.
