# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster-moving rivals. I'm glad to present our Q3 priorities, which came out of two weeks of planning sessions across every team.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision will change how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will mark this stage of the company the way the original launch marked the first one.

When every group wants its project first, the roadmap gets stuck. Leadership worked through a difficult planning cycle anyway and landed on three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases, even though our own infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

We aren't trying to do more. We're trying to do the right three things. We picked these bets for the revenue, and also for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll return to the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. On staffing, we should try something other than hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the new platform will connect every internal tool. The reliability push rests on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

The reliability workstream, owned by infrastructure, cuts across every product team. The platform workstream, owned by the core team, blocks two of the other three bets. The growth workstream, owned by product, depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports suggest that incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate seems to correlate more with deploy frequency than with headcount. The correlation may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. The sequencing bet could pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone may justify the quarter.

Reliability first. Platform second. Growth third. No exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard. We staffed the platform team. We paused two lower-priority projects. We told customers what to expect.

The result is a calmer on-call rotation and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. The platform budget is the largest single line item this quarter. Headcount for growth is the last lever we'll pull, only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

This isn't a hiring freeze. It's a hiring pause on anything that isn't reliability or platform. We approved these budgets for this quarter, and for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first budget line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second budget line covers the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. The third budget line covers a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is always contentious. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

Whether you're on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We're not asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less, better.

Some feature work slips to Q4 because of this, and that's the tradeoff. In exchange we're protecting the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

You may be wondering what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work will resume at full speed, and how we will know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

This plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real but manageable, and painful but temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, and no scope creep. This isn't a pause on growth. It's the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

This sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat. We have three bets, one quarter, and full team alignment.

As we discussed at the offsite, low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't get us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and put more into growth once it's finished.
