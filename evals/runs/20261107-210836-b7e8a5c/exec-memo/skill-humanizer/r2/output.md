# Q3 strategy memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter we spend without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster rivals. After two weeks of planning sessions with every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where we stand

What we decide this quarter will change how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will matter as much to this period of the company as the original launch did to the first one.

Planning gets hard when every group wants its project to go first. Leadership worked through a difficult planning cycle and came out with three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases, and our infrastructure can't keep up with that pace yet.

We chose to do three things well rather than do more. Revenue was one reason for picking these bets. The other was that they force us to finish the platform work.

We'll review the metrics we care about at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to rethink how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. On staffing, teams should look for new approaches instead of hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The three bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the new platform will connect every internal tool. The reliability push depends on the teams staying focused and following through.

Infrastructure owns the reliability workstream, which cuts across every product team. The core team owns the platform workstream, and two of the other three bets are blocked until it's done. Product owns the growth workstream, which depends on the other two landing on time.

## Why this sequencing

Industry reports suggest that incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Experts agree that platform debt compounds faster once a team passes a certain size, and we passed that size last year.

Our analysis shows the incident rate tracking deploy frequency more closely than headcount. The pattern may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. If the sequencing works, it could pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone may justify the quarter.

The order is reliability, then platform, then growth, with no exceptions this quarter.

So far we've shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect.

On-call is calmer now, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without going through another approval round. The platform budget is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

We aren't freezing hiring. We're pausing hiring for anything outside reliability and platform. We approved these budgets for this quarter and also for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first budget line pays for infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. The third pays for a contractor to handle the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is always contentious, and this quarter every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What this means for your team

This sequencing changes what "done" means this quarter, whether you work on a product team or a platform team. We aren't asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less and do it better.

The tradeoff is that some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange, we protect the platform work that will make every later quarter faster.

Your team will probably want to know what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work will resume at full speed, and how we'll know whether the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and once you see the dependency chain, the sequencing is simple. The tradeoff is real but manageable, and if the platform work lands on schedule, the pain is temporary.

## Bottom line

Q3 is about discipline: three bets, clear owners, and no scope creep. We aren't pausing growth. We're doing the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

We chose this sequencing on purpose, and it isn't a retreat. That means three bets in one quarter, with the full team aligned behind them.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't carry us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and put more into growth once it's finished.
