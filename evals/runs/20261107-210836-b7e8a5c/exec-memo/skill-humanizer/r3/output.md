# Q3 strategy memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter we spend without a clear bet is a quarter that faster-moving rivals take from us. After two weeks of planning sessions with every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where we stand

What we decide this quarter will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this period of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

Planning gets hard when every group wants its own project to go first, and this cycle was no exception. Leadership still got the list down from ten bets to three. The market wants faster releases, and our infrastructure can't keep up yet.

We aren't trying to do more. We're trying to do the right three things. Revenue was one reason we chose these bets, but we also chose them because they force us to finish the platform work.

We'll review the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to rethink how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. On staffing, teams should look at options other than hiring more of the roles we already have.

## The three bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents in half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that has sat half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier that three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration is done, the new platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push depends on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns the reliability workstream, which touches every product team. The core team owns the platform workstream, and two of the other three bets are blocked until it lands. Product owns the growth workstream, which depends on both of the others finishing on time.

## Why this sequencing

Industry reports suggest that once incident rates rise above our current baseline, they start costing renewals within two quarters. Experts also say platform debt compounds faster after a team passes a certain size. We passed it last year.

Our analysis suggests the incident rate tracks deploy frequency more closely than headcount. The correlation may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. The sequencing bet could pay off by Q4, and you could argue the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

The order is reliability, then platform, then growth, with no exceptions this quarter.

We've already shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. On-call is calmer now, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another round of approval. The platform budget is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

We haven't frozen hiring. We've paused hiring for anything outside reliability and platform. We approved these budgets with this quarter in mind, and also with an eye to the precedent they set for next year.

The first budget line pays for infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for the two platform engineers we're adding. The third pays for a contractor on the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is always contentious, and this quarter every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What this means for your team

Whether you're on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We aren't asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less and do it better.

The tradeoff is that some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange, we protect the platform work that makes every later quarter faster.

You'll likely want to know what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work will return to full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, but last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real but manageable. It's painful, but temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, and no scope creep. We aren't pausing growth. We're doing the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

We chose this sequencing deliberately, and it isn't a retreat. It comes down to three bets in one quarter, with the whole team aligned.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't carry us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and put more into growth once it's finished.
