# Q3 strategy memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter we spend without a clear bet is a quarter that faster-moving rivals use against us. After two weeks of planning sessions with every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where we stand

What we decide this quarter will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will matter as much to this period of the company as the original launch did to the first one.

Every group wants its project to go first, and the roadmap keeps running into that. Planning was hard this cycle, but leadership got the list down to three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases, and our infrastructure can't keep up with that yet.

Doing more wouldn't help. Doing the right three things will. We chose these bets for revenue and also for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll review the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. On staffing, the team should look for new approaches instead of hiring more of the roles we already have.

## The three bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that has sat half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier that three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the new platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push depends on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns the reliability workstream, which touches every product team. The core team owns the platform workstream, and two of the other three bets are blocked on it. Product owns the growth workstream, which can only land on time if the other two do.

## Why this sequencing

Industry reports suggest that once incident rates rise above our current baseline, renewals start to suffer within two quarters. Experts also say platform debt compounds faster after a team passes a certain size. We passed it last year.

Our analysis suggests the incident rate tracks deploy frequency more closely than headcount. The correlation may hold for every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. If the sequencing works, it could pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone may justify the quarter.

The order is reliability, then platform, then growth, with no exceptions this quarter.

We've already shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. On-call is calmer as a result, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. The platform budget is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

We aren't freezing hiring. We're pausing any hiring outside reliability and platform. These budgets cover this quarter, and we also approved them for the precedent they set for next year.

The first line pays for infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. The third pays for a contractor on the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is always contentious, and this quarter every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What this means for your team

Whether you work on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We aren't asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less and do it better.

The tradeoff is that some feature work slips to Q4. In exchange, we protect the platform work that makes every later quarter faster.

You probably have questions. What changes for engineering this quarter? Why does platform work come before growth? When will feature work return to full speed? How will we know the bet paid off?

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real but manageable. It's painful, and it's temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, and no scope creep. We aren't pausing growth. We're doing the platform work that next year's growth depends on.

This sequencing is deliberate, and it isn't a retreat. We have three bets, one quarter, and the whole team aligned.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't carry us through the next stage of growth. We'll take on the harder platform work now and push harder on growth once it's done.
