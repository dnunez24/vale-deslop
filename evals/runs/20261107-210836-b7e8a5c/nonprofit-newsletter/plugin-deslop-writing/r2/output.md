# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

This season Clearwater Relief finished its 500th well. Our wells now span four countries and serve roughly 120,000 people, and each one changes a family's future.

## What We Accomplished This Season

We expect this milestone to change how three regions approach clean water access. Well #500 will mark this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 marked the first.

The dry season was hard, but our field teams still finished twelve wells in a single month. Experts agree that when clean water sits within a fifteen-minute walk, school attendance changes, for girls in particular. Studies have shown that each dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in lower healthcare costs.

Clean water changes a village. Every community we've worked with has asked us for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

If you give monthly, or if you gave for the first time this year, your gift reached a real family this season. 💧

## Stories From the Field

The numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year. This season was hard before the rains finally came, and I won't pretend otherwise.

Water in Mbita used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well, finished in March, serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working. This year's donors seeded the maintenance fund, which covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team. The team now hears something like it in almost every village we visit. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

That leaves questions we keep asking ourselves. For her daughters, what changed, and why does a five-minute walk matter so much? And how long since the village last went without water? How many other villages are waiting for the same thing?

## Looking Ahead

Our field coordinator links headquarters with every village crew. The maintenance fund is our insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, and it draws on three years of lessons about what keeps a well running.

The roadmap covers planning, drilling, and training in a single dry season, village by village. It's ambitious, but the method is simple, and the wells are built to survive a decade of hard use.

In every region we serve, the plan aims for clarity, accountability, and reach.

Some partners have called it "unrealistic" for such a short timeline. Our field data from the past three years says otherwise.

The plan is funded and ready. We picked the sites, trained the crews, and ordered the materials, and drilling starts in January.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift goes to three categories:

- **Drilling:** rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- **Training:** local technicians who keep each well running for decades
- **Monitoring:** follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

The idea is to drill once, train locally, and monitor forever. That's how a one-time gift becomes decades of clean water. The model is lean, and it gives each village what it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |
| Training      | $310K      | 500               |
| Monitoring    | $180K      | 500               |

Drilling, our largest line item, paid for every well this season. Training cost less, but it kept every well running past its first year. Monitoring was the smallest budget of the three, and it caught eleven failing pumps before they broke down completely.

We started with a single well. We now run a four-country program, and other nonprofits copy the model. Along the way we learned that drilling without training fails within a year, that monitoring catches problems training misses, and that local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
