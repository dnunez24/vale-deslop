# Clearwater Relief: Fall update for our supporters

A new well can change a family's future. This season Clearwater Relief reached 500 wells across four countries, and together they serve roughly 120,000 people. We're very glad to tell you about it.

## What we accomplished this season

We expect this work to change how three regions think about clean water access. Well #1 opened the first chapter of Clearwater's story, and we think well #500 will open the next one.

The dry season was hard, but our field teams still finished twelve wells in a single month. Experts agree that having clean water within a fifteen-minute walk improves school attendance, especially for girls. Studies have also shown that every dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in lower healthcare costs.

Clean water changes a great deal for a village. Every community we've worked with, without exception, has asked us for the same things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

If you're a longtime monthly donor, or if you gave for the first time this year, your gift reached a real family this season.

## Stories from the field

The numbers don't show what changed in Mbita village this year. I also want to be honest that this season was hard before the rains finally came.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. Now the walk takes five minutes, and children stay for a full day of class.

The well was finished in March and serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working, and this year's donors seeded the maintenance fund that will cover the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team. The team now hears something similar in almost every village we visit. She said that before this year, her daughters had never gone a full week without missing school.

Her story left us with questions we keep asking: why a five-minute walk matters so much, when the village last went without water, and how many other villages are waiting for the same thing.

## Looking ahead

Our field coordinator links headquarters with every village crew. The maintenance fund is our insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, and it draws on three years of lessons about what keeps a well running.

Under next year's plan, each village goes through planning, drilling, and training within a single dry season. The approach is simple, but it is ambitious, and it's built to survive a decade of hard use.

In every region we serve, the plan aims to be clear about what we're doing, accountable for results, and to reach more people.

Some partners have called the timeline "unrealistic." Three years of our field data says otherwise.

We're ready, and the work is funded.

The sites are picked, the crews are trained, and the materials are ordered. Drilling starts in January.

## Where your support goes

Your gift goes to three areas:

- **Drilling:** rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- **Training:** local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- **Monitoring:** follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it breaks down

The model is simple: drill once, train locally, and keep monitoring. That way a one-time gift can provide decades of clean water. It doesn't take much overhead, and it gives each village the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |

| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |

Drilling is our largest line item, and it paid for every well this season. Training costs less, but it kept every well running past its first year. Monitoring is the smallest budget of the three, and it caught eleven pumps before they failed completely.

We started with a single well, grew into a four-country program, and now other nonprofits copy our model. Along the way we learned three things. Drilling without training fails within a year. Monitoring catches problems that training alone misses. And local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
