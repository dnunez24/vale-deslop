# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

Every well we drill changes a family's future. This season Clearwater Relief reached 500 wells across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people, and we're glad to tell you about it.

## What We Accomplished This Season

Three regions will think differently about clean water access because of this. Well #500 will mark this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 marked the first.

Our field teams worked through the dry season and finished twelve wells in a single month. Experts agree that access to clean water within a fifteen-minute walk changes school attendance for girls in particular. Studies have shown that every dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water changes a village. Every community we've worked with has asked for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Whether you're a longtime monthly donor or gave for the first time this year, your gift reached a real family this season. 💧

## Stories From the Field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year. I want to be honest about how hard this season was before the rains finally came.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well, finished in March, serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working. This year's donors seeded the maintenance fund, which covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team. The team now hears the same thing in almost every village we visit. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

What changed for her daughters? Why does a five-minute walk matter so much? When did the village last go without water? How many other villages are waiting for the same thing?

## Looking Ahead

Our field coordinator links headquarters with every village crew. The maintenance fund insures against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, built on three years of lessons about what keeps a well running.

Next year we move from planning to drilling to training in a single dry season, village by village. The approach is simple but ambitious, and light but durable enough to survive a decade of hard use.

The plan aims for clarity, accountability, and reach in every region we serve.

Some partners have called the approach "unrealistic" for such a short timeline, though our field data over three years says otherwise.

We're ready, funded, and moving.

We picked the sites. We trained the crews. We ordered the materials. We start drilling in January.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift breaks down into three categories:

- **Drilling:** rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- **Training:** local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- **Monitoring:** the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

The model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. Every village gets the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |
| Training      | $310K      | 500               |
| Monitoring    | $180K      | 500               |

Drilling, our largest line item, funded every well this season. Training, smaller but critical, kept every well running past year one. Monitoring, the smallest of the three, caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We started with a single well, grew into a four-country program, and now other nonprofits copy our model. We learned three lessons. Drilling without training fails within a year. Monitoring catches problems training alone misses. Local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
