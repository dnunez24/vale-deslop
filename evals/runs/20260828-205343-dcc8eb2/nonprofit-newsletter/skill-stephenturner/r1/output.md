# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

Clearwater Relief crossed a milestone this season: 500 wells across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people. Each of those wells changes a family's entire future.

## What We Accomplished This Season

Three regions will think about clean water access differently after this. Well #500 does for this chapter of Clearwater's story what well #1 did for the first one.

Our field teams worked straight through the dry season and finished twelve wells in a single month. Water within a fifteen-minute walk changes school attendance, and it changes it most for girls. Every dollar spent on water infrastructure comes back as four dollars a community never has to spend on healthcare.

Clean water changes everything for a village. Every community we've worked with has asked us for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Your gift reached a real family this season, whether you've given monthly for years or gave for the first time this year.

## Stories From the Field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year. The season was hard before the rains finally came.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well, finished in March, serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working. The maintenance fund, seeded by this year's donors, covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team. The team hears some version of that in almost every village we visit now. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

Her daughters got their school year back, and a five-minute walk is most of the reason. Other villages are still waiting for the same thing.

## Looking Ahead

Our field coordinator is how headquarters and every village crew stay in contact. The maintenance fund is insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious, and it comes out of three years of lessons about what actually keeps a well running.

Next year we plan, drill, and train in a single dry season, village by village. The approach is simple and ambitious at once, light to run and durable enough to survive a decade of hard use.

In every region we serve, the plan is judged on clarity, accountability, and reach.

Some partners have called the approach "unrealistic" for such a short timeline. Three years of our field data says otherwise.

We're ready and funded, and we're moving.

We've picked the sites, trained the crews, and ordered the materials. Drilling starts in January.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift splits three ways:

- Drilling covers rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- Training pays the local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- Monitoring buys the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

The model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water, and the approach is simple: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. It is light to run and still complete, and it gives every village what it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |

The drilling budget, our largest line item, funded every well this season. Training costs less and matters as much: it kept every well running past year one. Monitoring is the smallest of the three, and it caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We started with a single well, grew into a four-country program, and ended up with a model other nonprofits now copy. What we learned along the way: drilling without training fails within a year, monitoring catches problems training alone misses, and local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
