# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

Every well we drill changes a family's future. Clearwater Relief crossed a milestone this season: 500 wells across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people.

## What We Accomplished This Season

Three regions have changed how they think about clean water access. Well #500 will define this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 defined the first one.

Our field teams pushed through the dry season and finished twelve wells in one month. Clean water within a fifteen-minute walk changes school attendance, and it changes it most for girls. Every dollar spent on water infrastructure comes back as four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water changes a village. Every community we have worked with has asked us for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

You may have given monthly for years, or given for the first time this year. Either way, your gift reached a real family this season.

## Stories From the Field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year. The season was hard before the rains came, and I want to be honest about that.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. It now means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well was finished in March and serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working, and a maintenance fund seeded by this year's donors covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year. Our team now hears that same sentiment in almost every village we visit.

I can't tell you everything her daughters' year held, or how many other villages are still waiting for the same well. Those are the questions we carry into next season.

## Looking Ahead

Our field coordinator is the bridge between headquarters and every village crew. The maintenance fund is insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, and it reflects three years of lessons about what keeps a well running.

The roadmap moves village by village, from planning through drilling to training in a single dry season. It is simple and ambitious at once, light enough to move fast and durable enough to survive a decade of hard use.

We built the plan around clarity, accountability, and reach in every region we serve.

Some partners have called the approach "unrealistic" for such a short timeline. Our field data over three years says otherwise.

The plan is ready, funded, and moving. We picked the sites, trained the crews, and ordered the materials. Drilling starts in January.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift breaks down into three real categories:

- Drilling: rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- Training: local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- Monitoring: the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

The model is efficient. It turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water, and it is simple to describe: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. It stays lightweight without leaving anything out, so every village gets the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |
| Training      | $310K      | 500               |
| Monitoring    | $180K      | 500               |

Drilling, our largest line item, funded every well this season. Training cost less and mattered as much, since it kept every well running past year one. Monitoring, the smallest of the three, caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We started with a single well, grew into a four-country program, and built a model other nonprofits now copy. We learned that drilling without training fails within a year, that monitoring catches problems training alone misses, and that local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
