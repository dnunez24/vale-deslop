# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

Clearwater Relief crossed 500 wells this season, across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people. We have wanted to write that sentence for a long time. Every well we drill changes a family's entire future, and now there are five hundred of them.

## What We Accomplished This Season

Three regions now think differently about clean water access, and well #500 will mark this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 marked the first one.

Our field teams worked through the dry season and finished twelve wells in a single month. When a family can reach clean water within a fifteen-minute walk, school attendance changes, and it changes most for girls. The economics run the same direction: every dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water changes everything for a village. Ask the communities we work in what they want and you get the same three answers every time: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Your gift reached a real family this season, whether you have given every month for years or sent us something for the first time this year.

## Stories From the Field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year, and this season was hard right up until the rains came.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well, finished in March, serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working, and a maintenance fund seeded by this year's donors covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team. Our team hears that in almost every village it visits now. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

The questions I keep returning to are about her daughters, about what a five-minute walk buys back over a school year, and about how many other villages are still waiting for the same thing.

## Looking Ahead

Our field coordinator connects headquarters to every village crew. The maintenance fund is insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, and it comes out of three years of lessons about what actually keeps a well running.

The roadmap runs planning, drilling, and training through a single dry season, village by village. It is simple and ambitious at once, light enough to move quickly and durable enough to survive a decade of hard use. In every region we serve, it aims at clarity, accountability, and reach.

Some partners have called the approach "unrealistic" for such a short timeline. Three years of our own field data says otherwise.

We are ready, we are funded, and we are moving. The sites are picked, the crews are trained, the materials are ordered, and drilling starts in January.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift breaks down into three real categories:

- Drilling covers rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- Training covers local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- Monitoring covers the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

The model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water, and it is not complicated: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. It is a light model and a complete one, and it gives a village the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |
| Training      | $310K      | 500               |
| Monitoring    | $180K      | 500               |

Drilling is our largest line item, and it funded every well this season. Training costs less and matters just as much, since it kept every well running past year one. Monitoring is the smallest line of the three, and it caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We started with one well. We now run a program in four countries that other nonprofits copy. We learned first that drilling without training fails within a year, then that monitoring catches problems training alone misses. The last lesson is the one that reorganized everything: local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
