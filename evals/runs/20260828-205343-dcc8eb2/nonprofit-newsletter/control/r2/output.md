# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

Every well we drill changes a family's future. This season, Clearwater Relief crossed a real milestone: 500 wells across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people.

## What We Accomplished This Season

This milestone is already changing how three regions think about clean water access. Well #500 will define this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 defined the last one.

Our field teams pushed through the dry season and finished twelve wells in a single month. Experts say clean water within a fifteen-minute walk changes school attendance for girls especially, and research shows every dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water changes a village. Every community we've worked with has asked for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Whether you've given monthly for years or donated for the first time this year, your gift reached a real family this season.

## Stories From the Field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year. This season was hard before the rains finally came, and we want to be honest about that.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk, and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well, finished in March, serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working, and a maintenance fund seeded by this year's donors covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team — something they now hear in almost every village they visit. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

What changed for her daughters comes down to that walk: five minutes instead of two hours. How many other villages are still waiting for the same change?

## Looking Ahead

Our field coordinator is the bridge between headquarters and every village crew. The maintenance fund is insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. And next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, built on three years of lessons about what actually keeps a well running.

Next year's roadmap moves from planning to drilling to training in a single dry season, village by village. It's an ambitious approach, but a straightforward one, built to survive a decade of hard use.

The plan is built around clarity, accountability, and reach in every region we serve.

Some partners have called the approach "unrealistic" for such a short timeline. Three years of field data say otherwise.

We're ready, funded, and moving.

We've picked the sites, trained the crews, and ordered the materials. Drilling starts in January.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift breaks down into three categories:

- **Drilling:** rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- **Training:** local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- **Monitoring:** the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

This model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water. The approach is simple: drill once, train locally, monitor forever — giving every village what it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

The drilling budget, our largest line item, funded every well this season. The training budget, smaller but critical, kept every well running past year one. And the monitoring budget, the smallest of the three, caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We've grown from a single well to a four-country program, and to a model other nonprofits now copy. Along the way we learned three things: drilling without training fails within a year, monitoring catches problems training alone misses, and local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
