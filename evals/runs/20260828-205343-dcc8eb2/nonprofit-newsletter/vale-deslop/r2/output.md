# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

Clearwater Relief crossed a major milestone this year: 500 wells across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people. Each one changes a family's future.

## What We Accomplished This Season

This achievement will change how three regions think about clean water access. Well #500 marks this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 marked the first one.

Our field teams pushed through the dry season and finished twelve wells in a single month. Access to clean water within a fifteen-minute walk changes school attendance for girls in particular, and every dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water reshapes daily life in a village. Every community we've worked with has asked for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Your gift reached a real family this season.

## Stories From the Field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year. This season was hard before the rains finally came.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk, and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well, finished in March, serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working, and a maintenance fund seeded by this year's donors covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team, a sentiment the team now hears in almost every village we visit. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

What changed for her daughters? A five-minute walk matters because it turns lost hours into class time. The village hasn't gone without water since. And other villages are still waiting for the same thing.

## Looking Ahead

Our field coordinator is the bridge between headquarters and every village crew. The maintenance fund is insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, reflecting three years of what we've learned about what actually keeps a well running.

Next year, each village goes through planning, drilling, and training within a single dry season. The approach is ambitious for the timeline, and built to survive a decade of hard use.

The plan targets clarity, accountability, and reach in every region we serve.

Some partners have called the approach "unrealistic" for such a short timeline, though our field data over three years says otherwise.

Ready and funded, we're moving.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift breaks down into three real categories:

- Drilling: rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- Training: local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- Monitoring: the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

This model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. It gives every village the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

Drilling, our largest line item, funded every well this season. Training, a smaller line item, still kept every well running past year one. And monitoring, the smallest of the three, caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We started with a single well. Today it's a four-country program, and a model other nonprofits now copy. Along the way we learned that drilling without training fails within a year, that monitoring catches problems training alone misses, and that local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
