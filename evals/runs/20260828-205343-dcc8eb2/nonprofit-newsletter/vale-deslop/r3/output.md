# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

Every well we drill changes a family's future. Clearwater Relief passed 500 wells across four countries this year, serving roughly 120,000 people.

## What We Accomplished This Season

This achievement changes how three regions think about clean water access. Well #500 will define this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 defined the first one.

Our field teams pushed through the dry season to finish twelve wells in a single month. Access to clean water within a fifteen-minute walk changes school attendance for girls in particular, and every dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water changes daily routines for a village. Every community we've worked with has asked for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Longtime monthly donors and first-time givers alike sent gifts that reached a real family this season.

## Stories From the Field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year. This season was hard before the rains finally came, and it's worth being honest about that.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

Finished in March, the well now serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working, and this year's donors seeded the maintenance fund that will cover the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team, a sentiment the team now hears in almost every village we visit. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

What changed for her daughters, and why does a five-minute walk matter so much? The village hadn't gone this long without water before, and other villages are still waiting for the same thing.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift breaks down into three real categories:

- **Drilling:** rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- Training: local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- Monitoring: the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

This efficient model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. It gives every village the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |
| Training      | $310K      | 500               |

Drilling, the largest line item, funded every well this season. Training costs less but kept every well running past year one. Monitoring, the smallest share, caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We started with a single well; today we run a four-country program that other nonprofits now copy. We learned three things along the way: drilling without training fails within a year, monitoring catches problems training alone misses, and local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
