# Clearwater Relief: Fall Update for Our Supporters

Every well we drill changes a family's future. Clearwater Relief crossed a real milestone this season: 500 wells across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people.

## What We Accomplished This Season

This achievement will change how three regions think about clean water access. Well #500 marks this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 marked the first one.

Our field teams pushed through the dry stretch to finish twelve wells in a single month. Clean water within a fifteen-minute walk raises school attendance for girls in particular. Every dollar spent on water infrastructure returns four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water changes daily life in a village. Every community we've worked with has asked for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

This year, gifts from longtime monthly donors and first-time givers alike reached real families.

## Stories From the Field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year. This stretch was hard before the rains finally came, and it's worth saying so plainly.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk, and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well, finished in March, serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working, and this year's donors seeded the maintenance fund that covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team, a sentiment the team now hears in almost every village we visit. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

For her daughters, what changed? And why does a five-minute walk matter so much, when did the village last go without water, and how many other villages are waiting for the same thing?

## Looking Ahead

Our field coordinator is the bridge between headquarters and every village crew. The maintenance fund is insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, drawing on three years of hard-won experience about what keeps a well running.

Next year's roadmap covers planning, drilling, and training in a single dry season, village by village. The approach stays lightweight, but it's built to survive a decade of hard use.

The plan targets clarity, accountability, and reach in every region we serve.

Some partners have called the approach unrealistic for such a short timeline, though three years of field data say otherwise.

The plan is ready, funded, and underway.

We've picked the sites, trained the crews, and ordered the materials; drilling starts in January.

## Where Your Support Goes

Your gift breaks down into three real categories:

- Drilling: rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- Training: local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- Monitoring: the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

This model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. It stays lean, but it gives every village exactly the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |
| Training      | $310K      | 500               |
| Monitoring    | $180K      | 500               |

Drilling was the largest line item and funded every well this season. Training costs less but keeps every well running past year one. Monitoring, the smallest share, caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

Clearwater started with one well, grew into a four-country program, and became a model other nonprofits now copy. We learned that drilling without training fails within a year, that monitoring catches problems training alone misses, and that local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
