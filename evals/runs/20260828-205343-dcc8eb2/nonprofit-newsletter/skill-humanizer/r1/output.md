# Clearwater Relief: fall update for our supporters

Clearwater Relief passed 500 wells this season, spread across four countries and serving roughly 120,000 people. Every one of those wells changes what a family's future looks like.

## What we accomplished this season

We expect well #500 to change how three regions think about clean water access, the way well #1 opened the first chapter of Clearwater's story.

Our field teams worked through the dry season and finished twelve wells in a single month. When clean water sits within a fifteen-minute walk, school attendance changes, and it changes most for girls. Every dollar spent on water infrastructure comes back as four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water changes everything for a village. Every community we've worked with has asked us for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Whether you've given monthly for years or gave for the first time this year, your gift reached a real family this season.

## Stories from the field

This season was hard until the rains came, and the numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well, finished in March, serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working, and a maintenance fund seeded by this year's donors covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team, a sentiment the team now hears in almost every village we visit. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

Her daughters' school year, the five minutes she no longer spends walking, the last time the village went without water: those are the measures that matter here. Other villages are still waiting for the same thing.

## Looking ahead

Our field coordinator is the link between headquarters and every village crew. The maintenance fund is our insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, and it comes out of three years of lessons about what actually keeps a well running.

The roadmap runs village by village: plan, drill, and train inside a single dry season. It is simple and ambitious at once, light enough to move quickly and durable enough to survive a decade of hard use. It aims at clarity, accountability, and reach in every region we serve.

Some partners have called the approach "unrealistic" for such a short timeline, though our field data over three years says otherwise.

We are ready and the money is in hand. We picked the sites, trained the crews, and ordered the materials. Drilling starts in January.

## Where your support goes

Your gift pays for three things:

- Drilling: rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- Training: local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- Monitoring: the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

The model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. It is light to run and still gives every village exactly what it needs to keep a well working long after we leave.

Drilling is our largest line item, and it funded every well this season. Training costs less and matters just as much, since it kept every well running past year one. Monitoring, the smallest of the three, caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We started with a single well. We now run a four-country program that other nonprofits copy. Three lessons got us here: drilling without training fails within a year, monitoring catches problems that training alone misses, and local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
