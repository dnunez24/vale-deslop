# Clearwater Relief: fall update for our supporters

Every well we drill changes a family's future. This season Clearwater Relief crossed a milestone: 500 wells across four countries, serving roughly 120,000 people.

## What we accomplished this season

Crossing 500 changes how three regions think about clean water access. Well #500 marks this chapter of Clearwater's story the way well #1 marked the first.

Our field teams worked through the dry season and finished twelve wells in one month. Clean water within a fifteen-minute walk changes school attendance, and it changes it most for girls. Every dollar spent on water infrastructure comes back as four dollars in reduced healthcare costs.

Clean water changes everything for a village; everyone knows that. Less obvious: every community we've worked with, without exception, has asked for the same three things: reliability, training, and local ownership.

Whether you give monthly or gave for the first time this year, your gift reached a real family this season.

## Stories from the field

Numbers don't capture what changed in Mbita village this year, and they don't capture how hard the season was before the rains finally came.

Water used to mean a two-hour walk and missed school days. Now it means a five-minute walk and a full day of class.

The well was finished in March and serves 400 people. Local technicians run the training program that keeps it working. A maintenance fund, seeded by this year's donors, covers the next decade.

"This well changed everything for us," one mother told our field team. It's a sentiment the team now hears in almost every village we visit. She said her daughters had never missed a full week of school before this year.

What changed for her daughters, why five minutes matters so much more than two hours, and when the village last went without water are the parts of the story the numbers leave out. Other villages are still waiting for the same thing.

## Looking ahead

Our field coordinator is the bridge between headquarters and every village crew. The maintenance fund is insurance against the one thing that could undo a season of work. Next year's plan is our most ambitious yet, and it comes out of three years of lessons about what actually keeps a well running.

The roadmap runs planning, drilling, and training in a single dry season, village by village. It's simple but ambitious, light but durable enough to survive a decade of hard use. In every region we serve, it aims for clarity, accountability, and reach.

Some partners have called the approach "unrealistic" for such a short timeline. Our field data over three years says otherwise.

We're ready and funded, and we're moving. We picked the sites, trained the crews, and ordered the materials. Drilling starts in January.

## Where your support goes

Your gift breaks down into three real categories:

- **Drilling:** rigs, crews, and the materials that go into the ground
- **Training:** local technicians who keep every well running for decades
- **Monitoring:** the follow-up visits that catch a failing pump before it fails

The model turns a one-time gift into decades of clean water, and it's a simple one: drill once, train locally, monitor forever. It's light but complete, and it gives every village the support it needs to keep a well running long after we leave.

| Program Area | 2026 Spend | Wells Supported |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---------------- |
| Drilling      | $1.2M      | 500               |
| Training      | $310K      | 500               |

Drilling is our largest line item, and it funded every well this season. Training costs less but matters just as much: it kept every well running past year one. Monitoring is the smallest of the three, and it caught eleven failing pumps before they failed completely.

We started with one well, grew into a four-country program, and now other nonprofits copy the model. The lessons came in order. Drilling without training fails within a year. Monitoring catches problems that training alone misses. Local ownership is the only thing that lasts.
