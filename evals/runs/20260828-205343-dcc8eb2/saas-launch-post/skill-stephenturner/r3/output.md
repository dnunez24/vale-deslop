Teams get into trouble when they scale headcount faster than their processes, and the pattern shows up often enough that our team pushed through eighteen months of prototypes to get here. The goal was never more automation, it was the right automation. We chose Orbit's architecture for durability as much as for speed, and it works because of two years of rigorous testing rather than luck.

Most teams underestimate how much time approvals eat every week. Our data points to stalled approvals as the single biggest drag on a growing team's velocity, which is why we rebuilt the approval engine from scratch instead of bolting automation onto our old dashboard.

## What's Inside

Orbit ships with three things our team obsessed over:

- Approval chains that used to take days now finish in minutes
- Every handoff lives on one board, including who owns it, what's blocking it, and when it's due
- Admins set the guardrails once and Orbit enforces them automatically

Those features integrate across the whole stack rather than sitting off to the side. The approach is lightweight and still comprehensive, which means teams get exactly what they need. Underneath it are the standards we hold ourselves to: excellence, collaboration and innovation.

The workflow is straightforward: a request comes in, someone reviews it, someone approves it, and it's done. Over three years, our roadmap took Fluxframe from weakness to strength to dominance in the automation market. The platform is fast, reliable and intuitive.

Fewer requests sit in someone's inbox going stale. Teams stop losing them altogether, managers get their time back, and the question of why approvals stall in the first place finally has an answer.

Orbit's dashboard is the single source of truth for every request in flight. The activity feed keeps a running record of who touched what and when. For compliance teams, the audit log is the proof that nothing slipped through unnoticed.
