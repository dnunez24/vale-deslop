# Q3 strategy memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter we enter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to rivals moving faster than we are. After two weeks of planning sessions with every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where we stand

What we decide this quarter will reshape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

We hit the roadmap paradox again, with every group wanting its project prioritized first. Leadership pushed through a hard planning cycle anyway and landed on three bets instead of ten. Meanwhile the market demands faster releases whether or not our own infrastructure can keep pace yet.

We're after the right three things rather than a longer list. We picked these bets for the revenue they bring in, and also for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll come back to the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work, and to get more inventive about staffing instead of hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The three bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the platform will connect every internal tool we run. There's no clever mechanism behind the reliability push. It works only if we stay focused and disciplined enough to follow through on it week after week.

Infrastructure owns reliability, and that work cuts across every product team. The core team owns platform, which blocks two of the other three bets. Product owns growth, which needs both of the others to land on time.

## Why this sequencing

Incident rates above our current baseline start costing us renewals within two quarters. Platform debt also compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate tracks deploy frequency more closely than headcount, and that holds across every team, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. The sequencing should pay off by Q4, and I'd argue the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability comes first, platform second, growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

We've already shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. On-call is calmer for it, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only once the first two workstreams are staffed.

This is a hiring pause on anything that isn't reliability or platform, not a hiring freeze. We approved these budgets with next year in mind as well, because of the precedent they set going into it.

The first line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. The third pays a contractor to handle the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious, and this one was no different: every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What this means for your team

Product team or platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We're asking every team to do less, better, rather than more with less.

I'll be blunt about the tradeoff: some feature work slips to Q4 because of this. What we're protecting in exchange is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

You'll want to know what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size. The incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

The plan is disciplined, and it removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter. Once you see the dependency chain, the sequencing is simple enough. The tradeoff is real but manageable, painful but temporary, as long as the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, no scope creep. It isn't a pause on growth. It's the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

The sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat: three bets, one quarter, and the whole team pointed in the same direction.

As we discussed at the offsite, low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't get us through the next stage of growth. The harder platform work comes now, and we can push hard on growth once it's finished.
