# Q3 strategy memo: Anchorpoint Systems

A quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to rivals who move faster than we do. So here are our Q3 priorities, arrived at over two weeks of planning sessions with every team.

## Where we stand

The decision we make this quarter will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

We ran into the roadmap paradox again, the one where every group wants its own project prioritized first. Leadership pushed through a hard planning cycle and came out with three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases whether or not our infrastructure can keep pace, and right now it can't.

The goal isn't more work. It's the right three pieces of work. We picked these bets for the revenue they bring in and for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll come back to these metrics at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to step back and reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work, and to get more creative about staffing instead of hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The three bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the platform will connect every internal tool we run. The reliability work comes down to discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns reliability, and that work cuts across every product team. The core team owns platform, which blocks two of the other three bets. Product owns growth, which depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why this sequencing

Incident rates above our current baseline start costing us renewals within two quarters. Platform debt compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate tracks deploy frequency more closely than it tracks headcount. That correlation holds across every team, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. The sequencing bet should pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability first, platform second, growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

We've already shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. On-call is calmer than it was, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

Hiring isn't frozen. It's paused for anything that isn't reliability or platform. We approved these budgets for this quarter and for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. The third covers a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious, and every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What this means for your team

Whether you're on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We're not asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less, better.

The tradeoff is real, and I'd rather name it than bury it: some feature work slips to Q4. What we buy with it is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

Expect questions, and these are the ones I'd ask. What changes for engineering this quarter? Why does platform work come before growth? When will feature work resume at full speed? How will we know the bet paid off?

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is painful but manageable, and temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, no scope creep. Growth isn't paused. This is the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

The sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat. Three bets, one quarter, the whole team pointed the same direction.

As we discussed at the offsite, the low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't get us through the next stage of growth. We take on the harder platform work now and push hard on growth once it's finished.
