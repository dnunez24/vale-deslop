# Q3 strategy memo: Anchorpoint Systems

A quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster-moving rivals. These are our Q3 priorities, put together over two weeks of planning sessions with every team.

## Where we stand

What we decide this quarter shapes how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

Every group wants its own project prioritized first, and that fight shows up on every roadmap. Leadership pushed through a difficult planning cycle anyway and landed on three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases whether or not our infrastructure can keep pace, and right now it can't.

We kept the list at three on purpose. The point isn't volume, it's picking the right three things, and we chose these bets for the revenue and for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll come back to the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We also owe ourselves a harder look at how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work, and at staffing: hiring more of the roles we already have is not the only option.

## The three bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration lands, the platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push rests on discipline and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns reliability, and that work cuts across every product team. The core team owns platform, which blocks two of the other three bets. Product owns growth, which depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why this sequencing

Incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt also compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate correlates more with deploy frequency than with headcount. That holds across every team in the data, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. The sequencing bet could pay off by Q4, and I'd argue the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability comes first, platform second, growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. What we got for it: a calmer on-call rotation and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling. Infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only once the first two workstreams are staffed.

This is a pause on hiring for anything that isn't reliability or platform, not a freeze. We approved these budgets for this quarter and for the precedent they set going into next year.

In order, the lines cover infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard, the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on, and a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What this means for your team

This sequencing changes what "done" means this quarter, on product teams and platform teams alike. No one is being asked to do more with less. Every team is being asked to do less, better.

The tradeoff is real: some feature work slips to Q4. What we're protecting instead is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

You'll have questions. What changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan is disciplined, and it removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter. The sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The cost is manageable, and painful but temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, no scope creep. Growth isn't on pause. This is the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

The sequencing is deliberate rather than a retreat: three bets, one quarter, the whole team pointed the same way.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't get us through the next stage of growth. We take on the harder platform work now, and push on growth once it's finished.
