# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

A quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to rivals who move faster. Our Q3 priorities are below. They came out of two weeks of planning sessions with every team.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision shapes how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

Every group wants its project prioritized first, which is what makes the roadmap so hard to settle. Leadership pushed through a difficult planning cycle and landed on three bets instead of ten. The market wants releases faster than our own infrastructure can deliver them yet.

The point is picking the right three things rather than doing more of everything. We chose these bets for the revenue they bring and for the platform work they force us to finish.

We will review the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. Sequencing platform work against feature work deserves a rethink, and so does staffing, where the answer is probably not more of the same roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

None of this is a new idea. Once the migration finishes, the platform will integrate every internal tool we run, and the reliability push will come down to discipline and follow-through rather than any clever technique.

Infrastructure owns reliability, and that work cuts across every product team. Platform belongs to the core team; it blocks two of the other three bets. Growth sits with product, which needs both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt also compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate tracks deploy frequency more closely than headcount. The correlation holds across every team, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. The sequencing bet should pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability comes first, platform second, growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard and staffed the platform team. We also paused two lower-priority projects and told customers what to expect.

That buys us a calmer on-call rotation and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we will pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

This is a hiring pause on anything outside reliability and platform, not a hiring freeze. We approved these budgets with next year's precedent in mind as much as this quarter's spending.

The first budget line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on, and the third pays for a contractor to do the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

On a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We are asking every team to do less, better, not to do more with less.

Some feature work slips to Q4 as a result. In exchange we protect the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

The open questions are what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we will know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size. Last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple to follow once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real and manageable: painful now, temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, no scope creep. The platform work we do now is what makes next year's growth possible, so read this as groundwork rather than a pause on growth.

The sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat. Three bets, one quarter, and the whole team pointed the same direction.

As we discussed at the offsite, the easy wins got us this far, but they won't get us through the next stage of growth. We take on the harder platform work now and push hard on growth once it's finished.
