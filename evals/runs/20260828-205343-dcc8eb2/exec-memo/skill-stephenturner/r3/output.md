# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Two weeks of planning sessions across every team produced our Q3 priorities, and this memo lays them out. A quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster-moving rivals, which is why we spent the time.

## Where We Stand

What we decide this quarter shapes how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

Every group wants its project prioritized first, and that argument surfaces in most planning cycles. This one was difficult, and leadership pushed through it to land on three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases, and our own infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

Doing the right three things beats doing more of them. We picked these bets for the revenue they bring and for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll revisit the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. And staffing needs a fresh approach rather than more hires into the same roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push comes down to discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns the reliability workstream, and that work cuts across every product team. The core team owns the platform workstream, which blocks two of the other three bets. Product owns growth, and growth can't land unless the other two land on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports put incident rates above our current baseline at a cost of lost renewals within two quarters. Platform debt compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate correlates more with deploy frequency than with headcount. The correlation may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. If the read holds up, the sequencing pays off by Q4, and the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability comes first, platform second, growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, and paused two lower-priority projects. Customers know what to expect from us.

On-call is calmer now, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

We're pausing hiring on anything that isn't reliability or platform. That's a pause, not a freeze. These budgets cover the quarter, and they set a precedent going into next year.

One line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. Another covers the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. The third covers a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

The sequencing changes what "done" means for your team this quarter, on product teams and platform teams alike. We're asking every team to do less, better, rather than more with less.

Some feature work slips to Q4 because of this. What we protect in exchange is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

Expect questions about what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real and manageable, painful and temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, and no scope creep. The platform work is what makes next year's growth possible, so it buys growth rather than pausing it.

The sequencing is deliberate and not a retreat. Three bets, one quarter, the full team behind them.

As we discussed at the offsite, low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't get us through the next stage of growth. The harder platform work comes now, and growth gets everything we have once that work is finished.
