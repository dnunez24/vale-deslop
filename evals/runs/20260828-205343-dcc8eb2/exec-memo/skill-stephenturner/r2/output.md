# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster-moving rivals. After two weeks of planning sessions across every team, these are our Q3 priorities.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision shapes how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

The team runs into the same problem often: every group wants its project prioritized first. Leadership pushed through a difficult planning cycle and landed on three bets instead of ten. The market demands faster releases, even when our own infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

The goal is doing the right three things, not doing more. We picked these bets for the revenue and for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll return to the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We also need to reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work, and to staff differently rather than hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push rests on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns the reliability workstream, which cuts across every product team. The core team owns platform, and that work blocks two of the other three bets. Product owns growth, which depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate correlates more with deploy frequency than with headcount. The correlation may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. The sequencing bet could pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability comes first, platform second, growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect. That bought us a calmer on-call rotation and a platform team that can say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. The platform budget is the largest single line item this quarter. Headcount for growth is the last lever we'll pull, only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

This is a hiring pause on anything that isn't reliability or platform, not a hiring freeze. We approved these budgets for this quarter and for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first budget line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard, the second covers the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on, and the third covers a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

For product teams and platform teams both, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. I'm asking every team to do less, better, rather than to do more with less.

The tradeoff is that some feature work slips to Q4. What we protect in exchange is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

Your teams will want to know what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real and manageable, painful and temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, and no scope creep. The platform work is what makes next year's growth possible, so this is not a pause on growth.

The sequencing is deliberate rather than a retreat: three bets, one quarter, full team alignment.

As we discussed at the offsite, low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't get us through the next stage of growth. Let's take on the harder platform work now and commit to growth once it's finished.
