# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster-moving rivals. Here are our Q3 priorities, the result of two weeks of planning sessions across every team.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

Every group wants its project prioritized first, which makes sequencing hard. Leadership pushed through a difficult planning cycle anyway, landing on three bets instead of ten. Customers want faster releases, even when our own infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

This quarter is about doing the right three things. We picked these bets for the revenue they bring and for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll revisit the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter, and reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. Staffing should try a new approach too, instead of just hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

This approach ties the three bets together: every internal tool will connect to the platform once the migration finishes. The reliability push depends on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns reliability, which cuts across every product team. The core team runs platform, which blocks two of the other three bets. Growth belongs to product and depends on the other two landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate correlates more with deploy frequency than headcount. The correlation holds across every team, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. The sequencing bet should pay off by Q4; the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability comes first, platform second, growth third. No exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, and paused two lower-priority projects. We also told customers what to expect.

The result: a calmer on-call rotation and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability number is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Headcount for growth is the last lever we'll pull, only after the first two teams are staffed.

This isn't a hiring freeze. It's a hiring pause on anything that isn't reliability or platform. We approved these budgets for this quarter and for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first budget line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on, and the third funds a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

This sequencing changes what done means for every team this quarter. We're not asking anyone to do more with less; we're asking every team to do less, better.

Some feature work slips to Q4 because of this. What we're protecting instead is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

This raises a few practical questions: what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

This disciplined plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is manageable, and temporary if the platform work finishes on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, and no scope creep. This isn't a pause on growth. It's the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

This sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat: three bets, one quarter, full team alignment.

As we discussed at the offsite, easy wins got us this far, but they won't get us through the next stage of growth. Let's accept the harder platform work now and commit to growth once it's finished.
