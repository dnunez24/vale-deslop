# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

In today's competitive landscape, every quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster-moving rivals. Here are our Q3 priorities, after two weeks of planning sessions across every team.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

Every group wants its project prioritized first, which makes planning hard. Leadership pushed through a difficult planning cycle to land on three bets instead of ten. Customers want faster releases, even when our own infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

Doing the right three things matters more than doing more overall. We picked these bets for the revenue they bring and for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll revisit the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter. We need to reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. The team should try a new approach on staffing instead of just hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- Reliability: cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

This approach should produce real results, and the rebuilt platform will integrate every internal tool once the migration finishes. The reliability push rests on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns reliability, and its work cuts across every product team. The core team is responsible for platform, which blocks two of the other three bets. Growth belongs to product, and depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt also compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

The analysis shows our incident rate correlates more with deploy frequency than headcount. The correlation holds across every team, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. This sequencing bet should pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability comes first, platform second, and growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, and paused two lower-priority projects. We also told customers what to expect.

On-call is calmer now, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform has the largest line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, only after the other two teams are staffed.

This isn't a hiring freeze. It's a hiring pause on anything that isn't reliability or platform. We approved these budgets for this quarter, and for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first budget line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second pays for two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. A third funds a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is always contentious. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

This sequencing changes what counts as finished work this quarter, for every team. We're not asking anyone to do more with less; we're asking every team to do less, better.

Some feature work slips to Q4 because of this. What we're protecting instead is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

A few questions are worth asking directly: what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

This disciplined plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real, and it's temporary if the platform work finishes on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, and no scope creep. This isn't a pause on growth. It's the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

This sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat: three bets, one quarter, full team alignment.

As we discussed at the offsite, easy wins got us this far, but they won't get us through the next stage of growth. Let's accept the harder platform work now and commit to growth once it's finished.
