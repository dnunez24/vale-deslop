# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter without a clear bet costs us ground to faster-moving rivals. Below are our Q3 priorities, the product of two weeks of planning sessions across every team.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

Every group wants its project prioritized first, and that tension made this planning cycle hard. Leadership still landed on three bets instead of ten. Customers keep asking for faster releases, even when our own infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

We chose quality over quantity: three bets done right beats ten done halfway. We picked these bets for the platform work they force us to finish, as well as for the revenue.

We'll revisit the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter and reconsider how we've sequenced platform work against feature work. On staffing, the team should try a new approach instead of just hiring more of the same roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- Platform: finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- Growth: ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

This approach ties the three bets to actual outcomes, and once the migration finishes, the platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push rests on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

Infrastructure owns reliability, which cuts across every product team. The core team leads platform, which blocks two of the other three bets. Product is responsible for growth, which depends on the other two landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt also compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that size last year.

Our incident rate correlates more with deploy frequency than headcount. That correlation holds across every team, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. The sequencing bet should pay off by Q4, and the reliability work alone justifies the quarter.

Reliability comes first, platform second, growth third, with no exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, and paused two lower-priority projects. We also told customers what to expect.

On-call is calmer now, and the platform team can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability number is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, only after the first two teams are staffed.

This is a hiring pause, not a freeze: we're pausing everything except reliability and platform. We approved these budgets for this quarter and for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. Two additional platform engineers account for the second. The third line pays for a contractor on the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is contentious, as usual. Every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

This sequencing changes what counts as done for product and platform teams alike this quarter. We're not asking anyone to do more with less; we're asking every team to do less, better.

The tradeoff: some feature work slips to Q4 because of this. What we're protecting instead is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

What changes for engineering this quarter, and why does platform work come before growth? Feature work won't resume at full speed until that's settled. There's also the open question of how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

This disciplined plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real, but it ends once the platform work is done on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, no scope creep. This isn't a pause on growth; it's the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

This sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat: three bets, one quarter, full team alignment.

As we discussed at the offsite, easy wins got us this far, but they won't get us through the next stage of growth. Let's accept the harder platform work now and commit to growth once it's finished.
