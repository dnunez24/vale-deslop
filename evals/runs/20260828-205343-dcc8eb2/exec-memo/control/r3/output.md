# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

A quarter without a clear bet is a quarter we lose to faster-moving rivals. Here are our Q3 priorities, after two weeks of planning sessions across every team.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision will reshape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first one.

Every group wants its project prioritized first, and avoiding that trap took real discipline. Leadership pushed through a difficult planning cycle to land on three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases, even though our own infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

It's not about doing more. It's about doing the right three things. We picked these bets not just for revenue, but for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll revisit the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter, and reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. On staffing, the plan isn't to hire more of the same roles we already have — it's to look for different ones.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

None of this is glamorous, but once the migration finishes, every internal tool will run on the same platform. The reliability push comes down to discipline, focus, and follow-through.

The reliability workstream — owned by infrastructure — cuts across every product team. The platform workstream — owned by the core team — blocks two of the other three bets. The growth workstream — owned by product — depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry data says incident rates above our baseline start costing renewals within two quarters, and platform debt is known to compound faster once a team crosses a certain size — a line we crossed last year.

Our incident rate seems to correlate more with deploy frequency than headcount. That correlation appears to hold across teams, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. The sequencing bet could pay off by Q4 — and the reliability work alone might justify the quarter.

Reliability first. Platform second. Growth third. No exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard, staffed up the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect.

The result: a calmer on-call rotation, and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling — infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

This isn't a hiring freeze. It's a hiring pause on anything that isn't reliability or platform. We approved these budgets not just for this quarter, but for the precedent they set going into next year.

The reliability line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. Platform's covers the two additional engineers we're bringing on. Growth's covers a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is always contentious, and every team lead asked for more than they got this quarter.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

Whether you're on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We're not asking anyone to do more with less — we're asking every team to do less, better.

Some feature work will slip to Q4 because of this, and that's a real cost. What we're protecting in exchange is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

Engineering wants to know what changes this quarter. Product wants to know why platform comes before growth. Everyone wants to know when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though the incident data from last quarter says otherwise.

This plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real, but manageable — painful now, temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, no scope creep. This isn't a pause on growth — it's the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

This sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat: three bets, one quarter, full team alignment.

As we discussed at the offsite, low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't get us through the next stage of growth. Let's lean into the harder platform work now and double down on growth once it's finished.
