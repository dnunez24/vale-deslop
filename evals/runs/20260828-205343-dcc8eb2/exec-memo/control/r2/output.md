# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter we go without a clear bet, we lose ground to faster-moving competitors. Here are our Q3 priorities, the product of two weeks of planning sessions across every team.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision will shape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first.

Every group wants its project prioritized first — that's the usual roadmap fight. Leadership pushed through a hard planning cycle anyway and landed on three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases even though our infrastructure can't keep pace yet.

This is about doing the right three things, not more things. We picked these bets partly for revenue, and partly for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll revisit the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter, and reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. Staffing needs a different approach too, not just hiring more of the roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

The payoff is real: once the migration finishes, our platform will connect every internal tool cleanly. The reliability push itself just comes down to discipline, focus, and follow-through.

The reliability workstream — owned by infrastructure — cuts across every product team. The platform workstream — owned by the core team — blocks two of the other three bets. The growth workstream — owned by product — depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry data suggests incident rates above our current baseline start costing renewals within two quarters. Platform debt is also known to compound faster past a certain team size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate correlates more with deploy frequency than headcount. That correlation seems to hold across every team, though one team's numbers may be skewing the average. The sequencing bet could pay off by Q4 — and the reliability work alone might justify the quarter.

Reliability first, platform second, growth third — no exceptions this quarter.

We've shipped the incident dashboard, staffed the platform team, paused two lower-priority projects, and told customers what to expect.

The result so far: a calmer on-call rotation, and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling — infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. Platform is the largest line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

This isn't a hiring freeze so much as a pause on anything outside reliability or platform. We approved these budgets with next year's precedent in mind too, not just this quarter.

The first line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard; the second covers the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on; the third covers a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is always contentious, and this one was no different — every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

Whether you're on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means this quarter. We're not asking anyone to do more with less — we're asking every team to do less, better.

To be honest about the tradeoff: some feature work slips to Q4 because of this. What we're protecting instead is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

The questions worth asking are the obvious ones: what changes for engineering this quarter, why platform comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

The plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real, but manageable — painful now, temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, no scope creep. This isn't a pause on growth — it's the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

This sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat — three bets, one quarter, full team alignment.

As we discussed at the offsite, low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't carry us through the next stage of growth. Let's tackle the harder platform work now, and go all-in on growth once it's done.
