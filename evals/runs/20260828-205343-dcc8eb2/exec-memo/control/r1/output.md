# Q3 Strategy Memo: Anchorpoint Systems

Every quarter we go without a clear bet, faster-moving rivals close the gap. After two weeks of planning sessions across every team, here are our Q3 priorities.

## Where We Stand

This quarter's decision will reshape how Anchorpoint competes for the next three years. The platform rebuild will define this era of the company the way the original launch defined the first.

Every group wants its project prioritized first, the usual roadmap paradox. Leadership worked through a hard planning cycle anyway and landed on three bets instead of ten. The market wants faster releases even though our infrastructure isn't ready for that pace yet.

The goal isn't doing more work, it's doing the right three things. We picked these bets for the revenue they bring and for the platform work they force us to finish.

We'll revisit the metrics that matter at every leadership sync this quarter, and reconsider how we've been sequencing platform work against feature work. On staffing, we need fresh thinking instead of just hiring more of the roles we already have.

## The Three Bets

Our three priorities this quarter, in order:

- **Reliability:** cut customer-facing incidents by half before the next renewal cycle
- **Platform:** finish the migration that's been half-done for a year
- **Growth:** ship the enterprise tier three customers have already asked for by name

Once the migration finishes, the new platform will integrate every internal tool. The reliability push rests on discipline, focus, and follow-through.

The reliability workstream, owned by infrastructure, cuts across every product team. The platform workstream, owned by the core team, blocks two of the other three bets. The growth workstream, owned by product, depends on both of the others landing on time.

## Why This Sequencing

Industry reports link incident rates above our baseline to renewal losses within two quarters. Platform debt also compounds faster once a team crosses a certain size, and we crossed that line last year.

Our incident rate seems to correlate more with deploy frequency than headcount. That correlation may hold across every team, though one team's numbers could be skewing the average. The sequencing bet could pay off by Q4 — the reliability work alone might justify the quarter.

Reliability first. Platform second. Growth third. No exceptions this quarter.

We shipped the incident dashboard. We staffed the platform team. We paused two lower-priority projects. We told customers what to expect.

The result: a calmer on-call rotation and a platform team that can finally say yes to new work.

## Budget and Staffing

The reliability budget is a floor, not a ceiling: infrastructure can spend up to it without another approval round. The platform budget is the largest single line item this quarter. Growth headcount is the last lever we'll pull, and only after the first two workstreams are staffed.

This is a hiring pause on anything outside reliability or platform, not a freeze. We approved these budgets for this quarter's needs and for the precedent they set going into next year.

The first budget line covers infrastructure's on-call tooling and the incident dashboard. The second budget line covers the two additional platform engineers we're bringing on. The third budget line covers a contractor for the enterprise-tier integration work.

Budget season is always contentious, and this quarter every team lead asked for more than they got.

| Workstream  | Budget | Headcount |
| ----------- | ------ | --------- |
| Reliability | $420K  | 6          |
| Platform    | $610K  | 9          |
| Growth      | $180K  | 3          |

## What This Means for Your Team

Whether you're on a product team or a platform team, this sequencing changes what "done" means for you this quarter. We're not asking anyone to do more with less. We're asking every team to do less, better.

To be honest about the tradeoff: some feature work slips to Q4 because of this. What we're protecting is the platform work that makes every future quarter faster.

That means answering a few real questions this quarter: what changes for engineering, why platform work comes before growth, when feature work resumes at full speed, and how we'll know the bet paid off.

Some team leads have called this sequencing "too conservative" for a company our size, though last quarter's incident data says otherwise.

This disciplined plan removes the ambiguity that hurt us last quarter, and the sequencing is simple once you see the dependency chain. The tradeoff is real but manageable, painful but temporary if the platform work lands on schedule.

## Bottom Line

Q3 is a discipline quarter: three bets, clear ownership, no scope creep. This isn't a pause on growth so much as the platform work that makes next year's growth possible.

This sequencing is deliberate, not a retreat: three bets, one quarter, full team alignment.

As we discussed at the offsite, low-hanging fruit got us this far, but it won't get us through the next stage of growth. We need to take on the harder platform work now, and put full effort into growth once it's done.
